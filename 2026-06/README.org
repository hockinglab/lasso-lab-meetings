- 2 June: Hakima Laribi,
  - Title: Synthetic Data for Accessible Learning in Healthcare:
    Predicting the Hospital One-year Mortality Risk with Admission Data
  - Abstract: Accurate prediction of medium-term survival after
    hospital admission is essential for identifying end-of-life
    patients who may benefit from earlier goals-of-care (GOC)
    discussions. Multiple studies have leveraged electronic health
    record (EHR) data to predict the hospital one-year mortality risk
    (HOMR), using information collected at different stages of the
    care trajectory, including at discharge, during hospitalization,
    or at the exact moment of admission. More recent work has also
    explored the added value of incorporating longitudinal patient
    history to improve prognostic performance. In this seminar, I will
    present a synthetic dataset (https://zenodo.org/records/12954673)
    generated from an original clinical dataset used to study the HOMR
    prediction problem. We reproduce the same experimental setup on
    both the original and synthetic datasets and compare the obtained
    results. I will also discuss additional research questions that
    can be explored using the synthetic dataset, as well as a
    previously proposed hands-on challenge designed to facilitate
    practical work with the data.
- 9 June: Toby, 10AM-noon, [[https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1mHTFR6Eg7OdKi6yJcAvMk5_B8hjtMmsczs8Ewxt2xT8/edit][Using and contributing to the data.table package for efficient big data analysis]]
  - Abstract: data.table is one of the most efficient open-source
    in-memory data manipulation packages available today. First
    released to CRAN by Matt Dowle in 2006, it continues to grow in
    popularity, and now over 1700 other CRAN packages depend on
    data.table. This talk will start with data reading from CSV,
    discuss basic and advanced data manipulation topics, and finally
    will end with a discussion about how you can contribute to
    data.table.
